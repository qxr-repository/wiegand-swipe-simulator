How it works

  1. Upload the sketch, then open the Serial Monitor (line ending: Newline).
  2. Copy the clock line on the right and send it. The Uno now knows the day and time.
  3. Around each schedule time, it swipes every test card on a fixed beat, before and after the change.
  4. In Qumulex, check that each Group's result flips at the right minute.
The Uno has no clock of its own, and opening the Serial Monitor resets it, so always send the clock line after opening it. Its timing can drift several seconds an hour; re-send the clock line shortly before an important edge. Daylight saving changes aren't tracked; re-send after one.

Keypad (PIN) output

Each key is sent as its own short Wiegand burst. Set the panel/door keypad format to match. 8-bit sends the key's complement first, then the key.

Format and timing

Larry used 40 µs pulses, 2000 µs apart. Typical Wiegand is 20–100 µs pulses, 1–2 ms apart.
Verbose adds the raw bits of each frame. Schedule and sequence tests always log, since the log is the result.
